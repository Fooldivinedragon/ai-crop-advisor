How it works

From six farmer inputs to a complete crop, yield and fertilizer plan, step by step.

The pipeline

  1. 1

    Farmer input

    Soil nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and pH, a district name or GPS coordinates, and the planting date.

  2. 2

    Location

    GPS is used directly. District names are matched against a table of 50 districts, or looked up with the Open-Meteo geocoding API.

  3. 3

    Season

    June–September is Kharif, October–January is Rabi, February–May is Zaid.

  4. 4

    Historical weather

    Daily temperature, rainfall, humidity and solar radiation are downloaded from NASA POWER. The same calendar window is taken from each of the last five years and averaged, giving the typical weather of the season.

  5. 5

    Crop recommendation

    A Random Forest model, trained on {{ ev.rows }} samples of {{ ev.crops }} crops, gives a probability for every crop. In-season crops come first.

  6. 6

    Climate check and ranking

    Each candidate gets its own weather window matching its growing period. The final score is model probability × climate suitability, and up to three crops are shown.

  7. 7

    Yield estimate

    Expected harvest for each of the five past seasons, giving an average and a good-year to bad-year range in quintals per acre.

  8. 8

    Fertilizer plan

    Soil status adjusts the standard dose, which is converted into DAP, urea and MOP bags with application dates.

  9. 9

    Simple explanation

    The finished report is sent to an AI language model, which explains it in the farmer's language using only the computed numbers.

System architecture

A Flask web server calls seven modules, each with a single job. Two external services supply weather and location data, and an LLM writes the explanation.

Yield estimation

The typical yield of the crop is reduced by seven stress factors, each between 0 and 1, so every loss can be explained.

Y = Ybase × fT × fE × fW × fS × fH × fN × fpH

FactorWhat it measures
fT temperatureSeason's mean temperature against the crop's optimal range
fE extremesCold or heat spells beyond what the crop survives
fW waterRainfall against the crop's need (irrigation fills the gap if available)
fS sunlightAverage daily solar radiation
fH humidityDisease risk in very humid seasons
fN soil nutrientsThe most deficient of N, P and K (law of the minimum)
fpH soil pHDistance from the crop's preferred pH range

Yield in t/ha is converted to quintals per acre (1 t/ha ≈ 4.05 q/acre).

Fertilizer advice

Soil status adjusts the dose

Soil statusSoil value ÷ crop needDose
Lowbelow 0.6Standard + 25%
Medium0.6 – 1.0Standard
Sufficient1.0 – 1.5Standard − 25%
Highabove 1.5Standard − 50%

Nutrients become products

DAP = P₂O₅ ÷ 0.46
Urea = (N − 0.18 × DAP) ÷ 0.46
MOP = K₂O ÷ 0.60

Quantities are shown per acre and as 50 kg bags. The basal dose at sowing carries all DAP and MOP; the rest of the nitrogen is split into urea top dressings at crop stages, with dates.

Data sources

📊

Crop data

Kaggle Crop Recommendation dataset (2,200 rows, 22 crops) plus 100 wheat rows built from real NASA weather of 18 wheat districts.

🛰️

Weather

NASA POWER daily data: temperature, rainfall, humidity and solar radiation for any location.

💬

Explanations

Claude, or the free tier of Google Gemini, restricted by the prompt to the numbers in the report.
